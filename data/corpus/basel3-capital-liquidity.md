# Basel III: capital and liquidity requirements

## Capital ratios
Basel III defines minimum capital requirements as a percentage of risk-weighted assets (RWA). The minimum Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio is 4.5% of RWA, the minimum Tier 1 ratio is 6%, and the minimum total capital ratio (Tier 1 plus Tier 2) is 8%. CET1 is the highest-quality capital, made mainly of common shares and retained earnings.

## Capital buffers
On top of the minimum, banks must hold a capital conservation buffer of 2.5% of RWA in CET1, which brings the effective CET1 requirement to 7%. A countercyclical buffer, set by national authorities between 0% and 2.5%, is built up when credit growth is excessive. Systemically important banks carry an additional surcharge. A bank that falls into its buffers faces restrictions on dividends and bonuses.

## Leverage ratio
The leverage ratio compares Tier 1 capital to total exposure, without risk weighting, and must be at least 3%. It acts as a backstop to the risk-based ratios, which can be understated if internal models underestimate risk weights.

## Liquidity Coverage Ratio
The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) requires a bank to hold enough high-quality liquid assets (HQLA), such as cash and central bank reserves or high-quality government bonds, to cover its net cash outflows over a 30-day stress scenario. The LCR must be at least 100%.

## Net Stable Funding Ratio
The Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) requires available stable funding to be at least 100% of required stable funding over a one-year horizon. It limits excessive reliance on short-term wholesale funding to finance long-term assets.
