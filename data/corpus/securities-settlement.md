# Securities settlement

## Trade date and settlement date
A securities trade is agreed on the trade date (T) and settled later, when the securities and the cash are actually exchanged. The settlement cycle is expressed in business days after the trade date: T+2 means settlement two business days after the trade.

## Settlement cycles
The European Union has used a T+2 cycle for transferable securities traded on trading venues since 2014. The United States moved from T+2 to T+1 for most securities on 28 May 2024. The European Union has decided to move to T+1, with a target date of 11 October 2027. A shorter cycle reduces counterparty risk and margin requirements but compresses post-trade processing, which pushes firms to automate allocation, confirmation and foreign exchange funding.

## Delivery versus payment
Delivery versus payment (DvP) is a settlement mechanism that ensures the transfer of securities happens if and only if the corresponding payment happens. It eliminates principal risk, the risk of delivering the securities without receiving the cash, or the reverse. Settlement takes place in central securities depositories (CSDs) such as Euroclear and Clearstream.

## Settlement discipline
In the European Union, the Central Securities Depositories Regulation (CSDR) settlement discipline regime applies daily cash penalties to participants that cause a settlement fail. Penalties are calculated per day of delay and are redistributed to the receiving participant.

## Back office and position management
After execution, back-office systems enrich trades with static data, confirm them with counterparties, generate settlement instructions and update positions. Accurate position management is required to compute profit and loss, risk and regulatory reporting.
