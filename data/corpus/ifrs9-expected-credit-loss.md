# IFRS 9: expected credit loss model

## Principle
IFRS 9 replaced the incurred-loss approach of IAS 39 by an expected credit loss (ECL) model: banks recognise provisions for credit losses before a default occurs, based on forward-looking information including macroeconomic scenarios.

## The three stages
Stage 1 contains performing exposures whose credit risk has not increased significantly since initial recognition; the provision equals the 12-month expected credit loss. Stage 2 contains exposures with a significant increase in credit risk (SICR) since origination; the provision equals the lifetime expected credit loss. Stage 3 contains credit-impaired exposures, for example in default; lifetime ECL is recognised and interest revenue is computed on the net carrying amount.

## Significant increase in credit risk
The SICR assessment typically compares the current lifetime probability of default with the one estimated at origination. There is a rebuttable presumption that credit risk has increased significantly when contractual payments are more than 30 days past due, and a rebuttable presumption of default beyond 90 days past due.

## Measurement
Expected credit loss is commonly computed as the product of the probability of default (PD), the loss given default (LGD) and the exposure at default (EAD), discounted at the effective interest rate and weighted across several macroeconomic scenarios.
