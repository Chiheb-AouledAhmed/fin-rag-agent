# MiFID II: best execution and investor protection

## Best execution obligation
Under MiFID II, investment firms executing client orders must take all sufficient steps to obtain the best possible result for their clients. The factors to consider include price, costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size, nature of the order and any other relevant consideration. For retail clients, the best result is determined in terms of total consideration, meaning the price of the instrument plus all execution-related costs.

## Execution policy
Firms must establish and apply an order execution policy describing, for each class of instruments, the execution venues used and the factors that determine the choice of venue. Clients must be informed of the policy and give their consent, and the firm must monitor its effectiveness and review it at least annually.

## Transaction reporting and record keeping
Investment firms must report the details of transactions in financial instruments to their competent authority no later than the close of the following working day. They must also keep records of services and transactions, including recordings of telephone conversations and electronic communications relating to client orders, generally for five years.

## Product governance
MiFID II requires manufacturers and distributors of financial products to define a target market of end clients for each product and to make sure that products are distributed to clients whose needs and characteristics they match.
